Ted leads the Digital Rivers Lab at the University of Wyoming, jointly appointed in the School of Computing and the Department of Geology and Geophysics. Ted is a hydrologist that focuses on understanding the quantity and quality of water resources over a wide range of temporal and spatial scales. His research is focused in 3 related directions:
- Using satellite remote sensing data to observe global hydrological patterns
- Developing low-cost, open-source sensors to expand observational data
- Building generalizable DL models for better understanding of surface processes.

Ultimately, his work aims to translate these observational and predictive tools into actionable information to improve human well-being and ecosystem management.

Ted grew up in Columbus, Ohio, and got his start in research as an undergraduate at Ohio State, leading a bathymetric survey of the Olentangy River, a small urban stream that runs through campus, to support hydrodynamic modeling. He went on to a Masters and PhD in Geological Sciences at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where his dissertation, *Advancing Remote Sensing of Fluvial Sediment Transport and Storage*, combined field methods and decades of satellite imagery to measure long-term riverbank migration and suspended sediment fluxes in large rivers around the world. He then completed a postdoc at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, developing deep learning approaches to sediment and discharge estimation, before joining the University of Wyoming.
